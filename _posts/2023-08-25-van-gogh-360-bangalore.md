The Van Gogh experience is immersive, but its not just because they put you in an AC cube with paintings projected all along. They warn you that the pretty pictures inside are earnest but failed attempts of a man to make something out of his life. The signboards inform you that he was a failure who painted many self portraits because he couldn't afford anyone to model for him, that the brilliant yellow hues of *The Starry Night* might be because he was poisoned by the very medicine he took to save his mental health. It tells the stories of a tortured man who's only steady support in life was his brother and who finally gave it all up after ages of trying.

On that bright note, the exhibition is *very pretty* and once you have taken your time to get all the clicks you want, awed at the paintings on the floor and finally found that one beanbag which is free to plop down on, you start seeing the immersive aspects. Little kids going wowww at the bright colors, running away from the spots on the ground as the colors change and the crowds cramming to get photos in, its even funny watching people finally have enough of the room and just leaving. That one kid who rushed at the screen, making beautiful wavy ripples all over deseves the special mention. His mother didn't look too pleased with that tough. The crowd comes in all shapes and forms, the dressed to the tee and the barely holding themself, together all appreciating at least some point of the show. As an artist, I think Van Gogh would have loved to see that scene.
