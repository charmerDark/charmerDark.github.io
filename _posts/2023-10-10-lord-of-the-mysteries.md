In the Light novel circles, Lord of the Mysteries occupies an exalted position. A genre definer and a trend setter, the novel is a best seller that has many of my friends raving about it. All these made me try the novel and I spill some of my thoughts here.

LoTM has a Victorian era steampunk setting with elements from SCP and Lovecraftian horror all glued into an absolutely delicious world building narrative that simmers into perfection over a thousand chapters. The plot follows a normal person from earth who somehow finds himself in the body of an inhabitant of a steampunk world with arcane mysteries and powers just out of reach of the common man. He is investigated by a member of one of the many magic secret polices of the world and immediately has to think on his feet to keep his freedom. Thus starts the saga.

The serialised medium of web novels provides authors both the space and incentive to flesh out the backgrounds and characters and Cuttlefish that Loves Diving (yes, that is the pseudonym of the author) takes his time to build these into the story telling. The main character starts off economically struggling and penny pinches his way throughout the first volume. Its almost inspiring seeing him crawl out of the economic abyss as many of his perceived altruistic actions are actually greedy grasps for money. Even further into the novel, it is very interesting to note his relationship with money evolving and to see him reminiscence about his poor days.

Another beautiful aspect of the book are many of its minor characters that are built just to tug on your heart strings. I can only feel bad for the original readers, who had to wait months for the next volume to come out before they could find out what is about to happen. All hail binge reading! The pragmatic sister, the caring and jovial brother, the informer, the little washer kid from the slum, the doctor, the pirate, the detective all latch on to the reader over the course of chapters, as more and more aspects of their personality are portrayed.

> "Is there a disaster like this every year, or maybe even more than one?" Daisy asked with a slightly ethereal voice with a tinge of innocence.

Same thoughts dear, same thoughts.

The novel introduces an intricate system of magic that is slowly revealed. The abilities are very varied and often bizarre and even more so are the magical "artifacts" that appear throughout the novel. There is a quill which makes anything written with it reality, but will start plotting and writing the death of its owner if left alone for a moment. There is a die that will role on its own and augment your luck if the roll is above 4 or ruin it below. A mirror of vast knowledge that works on the principle of reciprocity, it will ask you a very intimate question in front of witnesses that you have to answer with truth, or be smote by lightening as punishment and so on.

The plot pacing in each volume starts with elaborate slow paced descriptions of the various factions, systems of magic, gods, monsters and the pathways of arcane powers, which follow the Tarot Cards and then builds on to rapid sequences of events that keep the reader on an edge. Each volume of the book has a definitive ending which leaves you thirsting for more. Beyonders, as the magic users are called, are not the end-all all-powerful forces and often find themself on the back foot against the growing industrial revolution, which incidentally was ushered in by someone who had come to the world from earth too.

> The middle class's blind confidence in the Loen Kingdom's might, and the eager desire to raise their own positions… The intensifying conflict between different political factions in high society leading to a fracture… The king and his faction wish for a large-scale war… With all these factors combined, just from an objective standpoint, and considering this from the angle of materialism, it's almost inevitable for Loen to start a war

You know an LN is good when these many socio economic characteristics of a country are cited as the reason for war. And they are all expanded on over the book and so are the effects of the said war on all the strata of the society. This is all happening on the peripherals as an epic saga involving drama, action, adventure and fantasy brews in each page on the forefront. What more could you ask for?

Well, a good end would have been nice! As beyonder powers grew, the author seems to have fallen into the trap of making them too grand. Maintaining such an extensive system seems to have taken a toll on him and the chapters towards the end just didnt have the same shine. As the MC etched closer to the fabled higher powers I started feeling this was getting a bit out of even the author's hand. Abstract concepts like fate, errors, luck or mistakes had become powers and they seem to overwhelm the plot itself. Towards the very end, the only thing that kept me going was a visceral need to see this to the end and even there I was disappointed. The much awaited grand finale was not to be revealed in the book and would only come in the next book after another alternate plotline. No thanks. Was great keeping me company for a month or so, but not doing this again. If only the ending was more contained, this would have been a top notch fantasy epic saga.
